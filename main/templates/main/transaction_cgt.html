How this is worked out

The disposal is the credit side of the transaction: the asset that left an account. Its proceeds are what came in for it. Rands received are the proceeds exactly. A crypto received is valued at the rate for the day of the transaction — not today's rate — and that same figure is the base cost of the lot it created, so the two sides of a swap always agree.

The lots are chosen FIFO. A disposal consumes the earliest unmatched acquisitions still open on that account, oldest first, using each one up entirely before touching the next, until it is covered. A lot only partly consumed contributes only that share of its cost. Matching happens on its own, and is redone across the whole account whenever anything on it is recorded, corrected or deleted; this page only reads the result, so a disposal you untick Match FIFO on shows no base cost until you tick it again.

Each lot is priced on the day it was acquired, in one of three ways, shown in the Acquired with column:

  • Bought with rands — the cost is the amount that left the rand account. Exact, with no rate involved.
  • Swapped for another crypto — the cost is what the lot was worth on the day it arrived, which is the same figure declared as the proceeds of that swap. Declare it from its own row on the account, not from here.
  • Details lost — a one-sided transaction, with nothing recorded on the other side. The cost is what it was worth on the day it arrived.

The gain is proceeds less base cost, and it is a loss when it comes out negative. Where a lot cannot be priced — no rate stored for its date — or part of the disposal has nothing matched against it, that row says so and the page warns that the total is short.

These are balancing transactions. The holdings behind this ledger are a long-term capital investment, and a disposal like the one above is a small balancing move between them — shifting the proportion held in one asset rather than taking money off the table. It is declared as the disposal it is, in full and on the day it happened, even though nothing was cashed out and the value stayed invested.
