How this is worked out

Every disposal is listed once, in the year it happened. A disposal is a transaction giving up a crypto — sold for rands or swapped for another crypto, it makes no difference, both are declared. Buying crypto with rands is not a disposal, and neither is an asset simply arriving: they create a base cost that is carried until something consumes it, so they are not on this page. Rands leaving or arriving are never a disposal either — the base currency cannot gain against itself.

Each row is the same calculation its own page shows in full: proceeds are what came in, valued on the day; the base cost is what the lots FIFO consumed cost on the days they were acquired; the gain is the difference. Open a row to see the lots behind it and the method written out.

These are balancing transactions. The holdings behind this ledger are a long-term capital investment, and the disposals listed above are small balancing moves between them — shifting the proportion held in one asset rather than taking money off the table. Each is declared in full, on the day it happened, even where nothing was cashed out and the value stayed invested.

Nothing here is stored. The page reads the matching as it stands right now, so correcting a transaction changes this report the moment you save it — and a disposal you unticked Match FIFO on shows no base cost until you tick it again.
