How this is worked out

Every week of the history, R 1 000 arrives from outside and is put into the rand wallet or the Bitcoin wallet. It is money contributed, not money moved: it is never taken out of the rand wallet to buy Bitcoin, so every combination below is handed exactly the same amount and what separates them is only what they did with it.

Where it goes is decided by two moving averages of the Bitcoin price — a short one and a long one, the long being the short times a multiplier. The two plans are that one reading taken the two ways round:

  • Bitcoin on a rise — the short average above the long buys Bitcoin and sells rands; the short below it does the reverse.
  • Bitcoin on a fall — the opposite throughout: the short average above the long sells Bitcoin into rands, and the short below it buys.

One average is always above the other, so there is always a reading and neither plan ever sits a week out. Neither is the sensible one and the other the perverse one — which way round the history rewarded is the question, and it is answered by running both.

On the same reading, each week also moves a percentage of one wallet into the other: an up reading spends that share of the rand wallet on Bitcoin, a down reading sells that share of the Bitcoin wallet into rands. The share always comes out of the wallet it leaves, so neither can be spent past empty.

A fee is charged on everything that crosses between the two wallets — the weekly move both ways, and the contribution as well on the weeks it buys Bitcoin rather than landing as rands. It is taken off the amount converted, the way an exchange takes it. A week that only puts rands in the rand wallet converts nothing and so pays nothing, and a fee of 0% is a plan costed as though it were free. It is one figure for the whole sweep rather than a range: what an exchange charges is a fact about where the money is held, not a setting to go looking for the best value of. Move it and run again to see what a plan costs somewhere dearer.

The window is how far back the sweep runs, counted off the last day priced. It is one figure for the whole sweep rather than a range, for the same reason the fee is: a plan run over a different stretch of history is a different question, not a better answer to this one. The prices begin in September 2014, so asking for more years than there are gives the whole of them — the dates the sweep actually covered are printed with the results.

The sweep runs every combination of the two plans and the three ranges set at the top of the page, and ranks them by what they finished with. Weeks before the long average has enough days behind it to exist are not weeks the plan could have read, so each combination starts where its own long average does. A winner sitting at the end of one of the ranges is worth widening that range for — the best setting may well be outside it.

The prices are Bitcoin's daily close in dollars and the daily rand price of the dollar, multiplied together. They come from a different source to the ledger's rates, which are CoinGecko's and go no further back than a year, and they are deliberately never written into the ledger — a rand on a return has to be traceable to one source.

Every sweep is set against a baseline: the same R 1 000 a week put straight into Bitcoin and never sold, over the same weeks and paying the same fee. A ranking of plans against each other cannot say whether any of them was worth running, and this can — so a plan that comes in below the baseline lost money by being clever.

This is a reading of what has already happened, and the whole history is swept over with nothing held back, so the best combination here is the one that fitted the past. It is not a recommendation and nothing in the ledger acts on it.
